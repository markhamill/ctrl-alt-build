# Taking the CLI Leap with Antigravity

I will be honest: jumping into the command line for this project made me a bit hesitant at first. 

My previous attempt at building a blog inside Antigravity ended up hitting a wall, and before that, I had the whole Gemini CLI debacle to remember. When you have a couple of messy technical experiences in your recent memory, it is easy to hesitate before opening up the terminal again.

But that is not why I am building this. The whole point of CTRL+ALT+BUILD is to learn and build in the open, not to hide from obstacles or try to guarantee zero mistakes. So I took the leap and jumped straight in.

To play it safe, I ran a few extra `git push` commits along the way just to lock in known good states I could easily recover from if things broke. As it turned out, I did not need a fallback at all.

Working with Antigravity paired with 3.6 Flash was ridiculously smooth. Like, shockingly smooth. 

At no point were instructions misread or misapplied. I did not have to redo a single task, and perhaps best of all: there was zero copy-pasting code snippets from a chat window into my terminal, which meant zero human errors from my own sloppy editing.

It is almost dangerously fast. You can literally fly through application development and watch features change so quickly you barely see the code updating under the hood. 

Taking the leap was completely worth it. When the tooling clicks like this, building stuff is actually fun again.
